PATTERN READING

How to read chart patterns without treating them as signals

A chart pattern is a label for a visible arrangement of price movement. It can help organize an observation, but it does not guarantee a market outcome.

AI-assisted educational content · Reviewed for consistency · Not reviewed by an investment adviser · Updated October 24, 2026

A pattern is a description, not a promise

People use names such as triangle, range, double top, or breakout to summarize a shape on a chart. The name can make discussion easier, but two readers may disagree about where the shape begins or whether it is complete.

A pattern label does not reveal future prices. It should not be used as proof that a move is certain or that a particular decision is suitable.

Check context and scale

A cropped image can make an ordinary fluctuation look like a complete formation. Check the visible timeframe, surrounding price action, and scale before deciding that a pattern is present. If these are not readable, state the limitation.

Do not infer missing volume, indicators, or candle values. If a chart feature is not visible, it cannot support the interpretation.

Build a conditional hypothesis

Describe the shape and the evidence that led you to notice it. Then explain what would support the interpretation and what would challenge it. A useful explanation includes alternatives and uncertainty rather than presenting only the most persuasive story.

For example, a move above a visible range can be recorded as an observation. Whether it becomes a sustained breakout is a separate question. A screenshot cannot confirm what happens after the final candle or explain every outside influence.

Leave room for failure

Patterns can fail, look different after more data appears, or be interpreted differently by another reader. A responsible chart note makes those possibilities explicit.

  • Use “may resemble” when the image is ambiguous.
  • Separate visible structure from an expected outcome.
  • Do not convert a pattern into a BUY or SELL instruction.
  • Record what information the image does not provide.
