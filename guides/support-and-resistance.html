Support and resistance: read zones, not promises

Support and resistance are chart-reading terms for areas where price has previously paused, turned, or met opposing activity. They are not guarantees of what happens next.

AI-assisted educational content · Reviewed for consistency · Not reviewed by an investment adviser · Updated October 24, 2026

What support and resistance describe

Support is a name often given to an area where price previously stopped falling or moved upward. Resistance is used for an area where price previously stopped rising or moved downward. These are descriptions of past behavior on a chart, not physical barriers.

The terms do not prove why price changed direction. A chart may show that a reaction happened, but a screenshot alone often cannot reveal the participants, order flow, or news behind it.

Why zones are more useful than exact lines

Price can move through an area by a small amount, pause around it, or react differently on another timeframe. Drawing a single precise line can suggest more certainty than the data supports. A zone leaves room for the variation visible in the chart.

When comparing possible zones, prefer areas that are easy to identify from repeated visible reactions. Avoid adding many lines just to explain every small fluctuation. The goal is a clearer description, not a chart covered in predictions.

How to review a visible level

  1. Check the timeframe and how much surrounding chart history is shown.
  2. Mark the area where a pause or reversal is clearly visible.
  3. Describe the observation before explaining what it might mean.
  4. Look for evidence that could contradict the interpretation.
  5. Note whether the screenshot is clear enough to read the price scale.

A previous reaction does not ensure another reaction. If price moves beyond a zone, that alone does not show whether it will continue or return. Treat a breakout or rejection as a new observation that still needs context.

Limits of a screenshot

Images may be cropped, resized, or missing labels. A model can mistake a drawing tool for price action or misread a scale. ChartMentor AI should avoid assigning a numeric value to a zone unless the image makes the scale and level legible.

Use the beginner chart-reading checklist to keep observation and interpretation separate. The analyzer’s score describes image evidence quality; it does not predict whether a zone will hold.
